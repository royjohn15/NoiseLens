    Calibration

    Phone microphones differ by up to about 6 dB. Put a reference meter (or a calibrated meter app) next to the phone, type its reading, and tap Match.

    How the numbers are computed
    • Level: Hann-windowed FFT of raw microphone samples (browser noise suppression and auto-gain switched off). The power spectrum is weighted by the IEC 61672 A-curve and summed (Parseval). Shown with fast (125 ms) time weighting; the timeline uses the 1-second energy average.
    • Dose: NIOSH REL with a 3 dB exchange rate. Allowed time T(L) = 8 h / 2(L−85)/3; each second adds 1/T(L) to the dose. 100% = the daily limit.
    • Source: YAMNet (Google, AudioSet, 521 classes) runs on the phone on each 1 s of audio. Classes are grouped into 7 source groups by ontology; generic labels such as "Inside, small room" are skipped.
    • Trust: rule-based placement detection from tilt, motion RMS and step periodicity (autocorrelation), following Ear-Phone (Rana et al.). Seconds with a tap spike plus a loud peak are excluded; the dose holds the last trusted level for them.
    • Uncertainty band: ±2 dB when calibrated, ±6 dB when not (NIOSH and Jacobs et al. 2020 smartphone studies), widened when trust is low.
